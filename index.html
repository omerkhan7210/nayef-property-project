SEO scope

What's built in, what's separate, and who handles the rest.

Technical SEO is built into the site from day one. Ongoing SEO, the kind that needs strategy and fresh content month after month, is a different job with a different skill set. I'd rather say that plainly than blur it into this quote.

  • Clean URL structure and internal linking between services and related guides

Ongoing SEO, a separate track

  • Keyword research and content strategy
  • Writing or commissioning the guides and articles themselves

You don't have to source this separately if you'd rather not. I work regularly with colleagues who handle SEO strategy and ranking work on client sites, so if you want that side covered too, it can be picked up by people who already know this site, not a cold start with someone new.

Built for what comes next

The resources system, not just a blog.

This is the piece worth getting right before build starts, since it's what a later listings integration will plug into.

Taxonomy

Categories and tags at launch, so an article about tenancy law and one about maintenance costs can both surface under the right filters. Enough structure for a few hundred articles without over-building for day one.

Article template

One reusable layout with SEO fields and schema built in, rather than a one-off design per article. Related-content pulls from shared category/tag matches until per-article fields are added.

Archive

Filterable, paginated, built to stay fast at a few hundred entries rather than a few dozen.

If property listings arrive in a later phase, they plug into this same content model, categories and templates, instead of requiring a rebuild of pages that are already live and indexed. Extra fields for related content and per-article detail can be added on top of this structure whenever you need them, a quick addition, not a rebuild.

Questions worth answering now

What you're likely already wondering.

Anticipated rather than waiting for the call, so we can spend that time on decisions instead of logistics.

What happens to this site if you're not around one day?

This is the question that matters most on a site you'll own for years, so here's the direct answer. Next.js and Sanity are two of the most widely used tools in web development today, not something only I understand. The code sits in a GitHub repo under your account, hosting and the CMS sit under your accounts, and you'll get a written setup guide at launch. Any competent Next.js developer can read this project and understand it within a day, because nothing about it is personal or undocumented. The pool of people who could pick this up is larger than it would be for a one-off custom page-builder setup, not smaller.

Who handles ranking and SEO after the site is built?

The build covers technical SEO: schema, clean URLs, fast pages, editable titles and descriptions. Ongoing ranking work, content strategy and optimization, is a separate skill. I'm not solo on that side either: I work regularly with colleagues who handle SEO strategy on client sites, so if you want that covered, it can be picked up by people who already know this site, not a new team starting from scratch.

Why Next.js and Sanity instead of WordPress?

You told me the real priority is search visibility, AI citation and credibility, not just easy editing. This stack gets you a faster site by default, because pages are built as plain files ahead of time instead of being assembled when a visitor arrives. You still get an easy, no-code editing screen, in Sanity Studio, for every page and article.

Can I really edit everything myself, including titles and meta descriptions?

Yes. Every page and article has a title and description field right in Sanity Studio. No code, no developer, no deploy step, exactly as you asked for.

What happens if I want to add a new service category after launch?

A new service page follows the same template as the six built in Phase 2, so you or a developer can add one without touching the structure underneath. If you'd like it built for you post-launch, that's a small hourly task, not a re-scope.

What if a phase runs into revisions beyond what's included?

One structured revision round is built into each phase's price. Anything beyond that gets scoped and quoted as a small add-on before I start it, never billed after the fact without your sign-off.

Does structured data actually guarantee AI or rich-result visibility?

No, and I wouldn't want to imply that. Schema makes you eligible for rich results and easier for AI systems to read correctly. Whether you actually rank or get cited depends on content quality and competition, which is why ongoing SEO is its own track.

What does the resources library cost me to maintain long-term?

Nothing beyond what's already listed. Sanity's free tier covers this project's scale with real headroom. Publishing a new article costs nothing extra, no per-article fee, no plugin limit on volume.

What exactly is the 90+ Lighthouse commitment?

A 90 or higher score, checked on Google's PageSpeed Insights, on the live site with your real content and images already loaded, on the homepage, one service page, and one guide article. The final milestone doesn't release until it passes.

What exactly is included in the branding?

A logo, a simple color and type system, and templated business card/letterhead files matching that system, the same quality level as the examples I sent. It's built alongside the site rather than handed off separately, so the two stay consistent.
